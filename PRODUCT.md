# Product

## Register

product

## Users

A single player (non-technical, plays casually but likes skill-based games) launching the game in their browser for fast 10–20 minute survival runs. While playing they are in full-attention combat: mouse locked, eyes on the crosshair, decisions in milliseconds. Any UI must be readable in peripheral vision at a glance.

## Product Purpose

SKYBREAK is a 3D first-person endless wave-survival game with fast aerial combat (double jumps, air dashes, air-stalls) on floating sky islands at sunset. Five classes with opposite playstyles; eight enemy types with telegraphed abilities. Success = "one more run" pull: the player dies, sees their best wave, and immediately restarts.

## Brand Personality

Painterly, kinetic, heroic. The fantasy of a duelist skating across the sky at golden hour. Menus should feel like part of the world (warm dusk light, magic glow), not a website bolted on top.

## Anti-references

- Generic web-app UI (cards, gray panels, SaaS buttons) floating over a game.
- Cluttered MMO HUDs with dozens of bars and icons.
- Sterile esports/tech-shooter chrome (hexagons, scanlines, neon cyan-on-black).

## Design Principles

- **Glance, don't read**: in combat every element is understandable from shape and color alone; text is secondary.
- **The world is the menu**: full-screen states (menu, class select, death) sit over the live 3D world, tinted, never over a flat background.
- **Feedback is gameplay**: damage, cooldowns and charge states must be *felt* — flashes, pulses, screen edges — before they are read.
- **Quiet by default, loud when it matters**: the HUD stays minimal; it escalates (low HP, void warning) only when the player must act.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keyboard-only navigable menus (Enter/Space/arrows) plus mouse. High-contrast text over the 3D scene via scrims. Damage states communicated by shape/position as well as color. Reduced-motion users are playing a motion game by choice; UI chrome still avoids gratuitous animation.
